TVN view

Conclusion: this session was bad for holders of the pillars and neutral for the rest of the market; do not bottom-fish VIC, VHM and the banks yet; this conclusion changes if VN-Index closes back above 1,705.91 points.

  • This was a session in which the index and the crowd moved in opposite directions: 348 stocks rose and 301 fell, yet VIC alone took away 12.66 points, about 79% of the decline.
  • Put simply, VN-Index is weighted by market capitalisation, so it works like a scale on which VIC is the heaviest weight; a few people selling that weight is enough to tip the whole scale even while most people in the market are still buying.
  • Measured with our data, over the last 5 sessions the average HOSE stock rose 0.38% while VN-Index fell 1.81%; the 2.2 percentage-point gap is the part caused by the pillars.
  • Selling on the pillars came from two different sources: Vingroup stocks were dumped in the afternoon session and the ATC, while banks were sold because the market is pricing in the profit they must give up when cutting lending rates by 1.5-2 percentage points.
  • What the market can easily misread is treating the break of 1,700 as panic selling: the share of stocks closing above their average price is still 47.8%, far from the below-25% level of a panic-selling session.
  • Levels to watch for session 20/10: if the session low of 1,682.54 points holds and foreign net selling stays below VND 500 bn, this session was only a dump in the pillars; a break of 1,669.01, the 60-session low of 27/07, would mean the risk spreads market-wide.
  • The takeaway: the index fell because of the pillars, while the crowd has not panic-sold.

Governor meets 46 banks: lending rates to be cut by 1.5-2 percentage points, applied to existing loans as well

Sources: Bao Dau tu, Znews, Bnews, DNSE 19/10; document 9608/NHNN-CSTT dated 18/10
  • On the morning of 19/10, Governor Phạm Đức Ấn met 46 commercial banks to implement document 9608 dated 18/10: cut posted deposit rates by at least 0.5 percentage points for tenors of 6 months and longer, and cut lending rates by 1.5-2 percentage points for production, business and priority sectors.
  • The Governor said the rate cuts are "not one-size-fits-all", apply to existing loans as well, and that if banks keep pushing deposit rates higher, "there is little basis for cutting lending rates"; banks that do not comply may have their 2026 credit quotas reviewed and receive lower 2027 quotas.
  • On the banks' side, the state-owned banks announced lending rate cuts of up to 2%, BIDV set aside a package of about VND 100,000 bn at 6-7%, Sacombank a package of about VND 40,000 bn from 8.5%; LPBank, BVBank, VietBank, Nam A Bank and ACB cut deposit rates.
  • At the same meeting, the Governor said credit as of 14/10 had grown 11.44% from end-2025, below the 11.59% as of 30/09, meaning outstanding loans shrank by about 0.15 percentage points in the first half of October.
TVN view

Conclusion: good for borrowing companies and securities stocks, bad for bank margins over 1-2 quarters; do not add to state-owned banks yet; this conclusion changes if the State Bank cuts policy rates to give banks cheaper funding.

  • The two cuts are not balanced: lending rates fall 1.5-2 percentage points while deposit rates only need to fall 0.5 percentage points, so the 1-1.5 percentage point gap is absorbed by bank margins, and the market priced this in within the session as VCB fell 1.23%, ACB 1.70% and VPB 2.04%.
  • Why now: credit is shrinking while the 2027 GDP target is 10% or more, so a quick push is needed; cutting policy rates would act faster but would pressure the exchange rate, with the US 10-year yield around 5.085% while Vietnam's 10-year government bond yields only 4.47%.
  • Second-round effects: funding costs fall for real estate, materials and securities companies, while depositors will look for other channels, including stocks, though this usually takes 1-2 months.
  • For TVN & Partners' advisory work, borrowing companies should ask their banks to apply the cut to existing loans within October.
  • Key takeaway: banks are paying the gap of lower interest rates.

Draft amended Securities Law: removes the 15% minimum sale requirement at IPO, adds a sandbox mechanism

Sources: VnExpress 18/10; Press release No. 01 of the 2nd session of the 16th National Assembly

On 17/10, Finance Minister Ngô Văn Tuấn presented to the National Assembly the draft law amending and supplementing the Securities Law: it removes the requirement to sell at least 15% of voting shares to the public at IPO (10% for companies with capital of VND 1,000 bn or more), adds a controlled sandbox mechanism in Article 6 and eases conditions for custodian banks. Plenary discussion is expected on 28/10, a vote on 13/11, with a proposed effective date of 01/03/2027.

TVN view

Conclusion: positive in the medium term for new supply of listings and securities companies with investment banking arms, no short-term price impact yet; watch the revised draft after the 28/10 discussion.

  • Removing the 15% threshold lets large companies, including equitized state-owned enterprises, list without selling a large stake at once, like being allowed to open a store before clearing all inventory.
  • The downside is that free float of new listings may be low, a criterion FTSE and MSCI track; what the market has not yet noticed is that this could make it harder for new listings to enter indices.
  • Key takeaway: the new law opens the door for new listings, but they need enough free float to enter the indices.
