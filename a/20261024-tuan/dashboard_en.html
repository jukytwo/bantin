VN-Index rose 4% for the week even though foreign investors net sold VND 966.5 bn and Brent climbed to USD 116.72. So what money is driving the market?

The answer is domestic money, following the State Bank's order to cut deposit rates, not foreign capital yet.

🔎 MARKET VIEW: 5 POINTS TO NOTE
1️⃣ Cheaper money lifts the index

Official Letter 9736 of 21/10 requires banks to cut rates on new deposits with tenors of 6 months or more by at least 0.5% p.a. On that very day VN-Index rose 46.70 points with order matching of VND 17,071.1 bn.

2️⃣ Foreign investors have not returned yet

Two net buying sessions on 21-22/10 broke a 20-session selling streak, but they still net sold VND 966.5 bn for the week. They bought VND 957 bn of TCB but sold VND 644 bn of FPT and VND 542 bn of ACB.

3️⃣ The index is stronger than the stocks

VIC rose 10.0% and VHM rose 5.41%, while the 23/10 session saw 156 decliners versus 144 advancers. Our bank portfolio only moved sideways while the index rose 4%, and we acknowledge that reducing VIC was the wrong call of the week.

4️⃣ Oil is the biggest test

Brent closed at USD 116.72, the highest since 29/4, after a series of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. Expensive oil has led 100/100 Reuters economists to forecast a Fed rate hike in December.

5️⃣ Discipline over forecasts

A strong week easily tempts people to increase margin just when external risks are at their greatest. Setting action thresholds in numbers in advance, then sticking to them, is how to preserve capital through a volatile season.

Trading week review

19-23/10/2026

TakeawayConclusion: The VN-Index rose 4.00% over the week on domestic money chasing the order to cut deposit rates, not on foreign investors; equity allocation should be kept at 50-60%, raised to 60-70% only if the index closes above 1,800 points with HOSE matched value above VND 15,000 bn after the Fed meeting on the morning of 29/10, and cut to 40-50% if it closes below 1,760 points.

  • In the week of 19-23/10, the VN-Index rose 68.24 points (+4.00%) to 1,774.15 points, its first weekly gain after four consecutive losing weeks.
  • The weekly low came in Monday's session at 1,689.84 points (-16.07 points); the turning point was the 21/10 session, up 46.70 points with matched value of VND 17,071.1 bn, the highest of the week, on the very day the State Bank issued Official Letter 9736.
  • Four consecutive gaining sessions from 20/10 lifted the index to a close above its 50-session moving average of 1,772.2 points.
  • The HNX-Index rose 3.57 points (+1.38%) to 262.70 points; the VN30 rose 51.68 points (+2.79%) to 1,907.09 points; the UPCoM-Index rose 1.39 points (+1.11%) to 126.26 points.

Average HOSE trading value was VND 15,827.7 bn per session, up 20.3% from VND 13,152.4 bn the previous week; average matched value alone was about VND 13,391 bn, up 19.7% (TVN calculated as total minus negotiated deals; the 23/10 session is an estimate). Nhan Dan newspaper reported HOSE trading volume up 22.3%. Liquidity rising in step with prices is what was missing last week, when average matched value fell 13.3%.

  • Foreign investors net sold VND 966.5 bn on HOSE (Nhan Dan; summing the five sessions from daily data gives VND 970.7 bn); Market Times reported cumulative net selling of VND 969 bn, while Vietstock reported about VND 1,000 bn across both exchanges, of which HNX accounted for about VND 106 bn.
  • The figures differ due to scope (order matching only or including negotiated deals, with or without UPCoM); we use the Nhan Dan HOSE figure as the main number.
  • The two sessions of 21-22/10 saw net buying of VND 189.8 bn and VND 340.8 bn, breaking a 20-session net selling streak dating from 23/09, but Friday's session returned to net selling of VND 480.04 bn, almost entirely in ACB.
  • Largest net buying of the week: TCB VND 957 bn, MSN VND 391 bn, VIC VND 307 bn, HPG VND 92 bn; largest net selling: FPT VND 644 bn, ACB VND 542 bn, VCB VND 311 bn, STB VND 231 bn, MSB VND 140 bn, VJC VND 125 bn.
  • The full-week total for proprietary desks was not found; in the 23/10 session proprietary desks net bought VND 12 bn on HOSE, led by ACB at VND 240 bn, opposite to foreign investors (Vietstock).
  • Money concentrated in two groups.
  • The Vin group: VIC rose 10.0% to VND 236,500, VHM +5.41% to VND 64,300.
  • Banks rose unevenly: TCB +3.01%, SSB +3.10%, SHB +2.35%, CTG +1.14%, while MBB -0.25%, HDB -1.10%, VPB -1.63%; in the 23/10 session alone STB hit the ceiling and ACB gained 3.90%.
  • Steel was mixed: NKG +15.38% but HPG only +0.24%.
  • The weakest were PNJ, -7.18% to VND 17,450 after its EGM on 21/10 disclosed a Q3 loss of VND 258 bn, and FPT, -2.06% to VND 57,000 as foreign investors net sold VND 644 bn and US chip stocks were sold off.
  • Energy did not follow oil: Brent rose about 6% but GAS gained only 2.89%, BSR 2.47%, PLX 1.14%.
  • According to Vietstock, the top gainer on HOSE for the week was VPI +19.33% and the top loser was CRE -9.44%.

TVN view

Conclusion: The VN-Index rose 4.00% over the week on domestic money chasing the order to cut deposit rates, not on foreign investors; equity allocation should be kept at 50-60%, raised to 60-70% only if the index closes above 1,800 points with HOSE matched value above VND 15,000 bn after the Fed meeting on the morning of 29/10, and cut to 40-50% if it closes below 1,760 points.

  • The thread across the five sessions is very clear: on Monday the index was still falling, to 1,689.84 points; on Wednesday Official Letter 9736 was issued and the index rose 46.70 points in a single session; and the next two sessions held the momentum with liquidity not dropping off.
  • The reason money chose banks is funding costs: a bank is like a shop that buys its goods with deposits and sells them as loans; when the purchase price falls by at least 0.5%/year while existing loan contracts do not reprice immediately, the margin widens for a few quarters.
  • The Vin group carries a large weighting, so VIC's 10.0% gain and VHM's 5.41% gain contributed a significant share of the index's rise; in the 23/10 session alone VIC added about 5.9 points (Vietstock); the index is therefore stronger than the average stock.
  • What the market is getting wrong is treating this week as the return of foreign capital: foreign investors still net sold VND 966.5 bn, selling heavily FPT VND 644 bn and ACB VND 542 bn, with net buying concentrated in a single ticker, TCB at VND 957 bn.
  • Our own portfolio proves the point: the banking, steel and FPT names we recommended last week only moved sideways, about 0% on average, while the index rose 4%, because the rally piled into VIC, VHM and a few overheated names such as STB and NKG.
  • For the coming week, we believe the index may pull back for a beat around 1,772 points, but stocks will diverge sharply and follow their own stories; the condition for the rebound to continue is HOSE matched value holding above VND 15,000 bn per session.
  • Three checkpoints: the VN-Index holding above 1,772 points, breaking the 20-session high of 1,788 points and then 1,800 points, and the reaction after the Fed decision at 01:00 on 29/10.
  • This week the market rose because money got cheaper, not yet because foreigners came back.
