Lending rates cut by up to 2 ppts

Official Letters 9608, 9736 and rate-cut packages

TakeawayConclusion: positive for heavily indebted companies and real estate, neutral to slightly negative for state-owned banks' margins; hold private banks with cheap deposits, and do not add state-owned banks until Q4 results show margins have held up; this conclusion changes if the State Bank cuts policy rates, in which case the whole sector would be compensated on funding costs.

Source: CafeF 22/10 (Techcombank, VDSC), Elibook 22/10, Prime Minister Lê Minh Hưng at the opening of the 2nd Session on 17/10 (CafeF), Luat Viet Nam (Official Letter 9736).

  • On 22/10, Techcombank announced lending rate cuts of up to 2% per year for eligible customers through two programmes with a combined size of VND 40,000 bn: VND 20,000 bn for small and medium-sized enterprises and household businesses, and VND 20,000 bn for large corporates and financial institutions; the bank also cut deposit rates for terms of 6 months or longer by 0.5 percentage points.
  • This follows two documents issued within a week: Official Letter 9608/NHNN-CSTT of 18/10 required a minimum 0.5 percentage point cut in posted rates for terms of 6 months or longer and lending rate cuts of 1.5-2 percentage points for production and business and priority sectors; Official Letter 9736 of 21/10 changed the yardstick to the average rate on new transactions, compared against each bank's own level in the 11-20/10 period, and banks that fail to comply may have their credit growth quotas reduced or set lower.
  • According to VDSC (22/10), as of 14/10 credit had grown 11.44% while deposits grew 11.22%, narrowing the gap between credit and deposits to about VND 2.35 quadrillion; when L/C purchases and sales are included in the quota, SHB is estimated at 19.4% growth against a 10.5% quota, TPBank at 13.7% against 11%, and Techcombank at about 15% against 12%.
  • Lending for production and business accounts for about 55% of total sector loans, highest at VietinBank at 66%, MB at 57%, Vietcombank at 56% and BIDV at 51%; Techcombank, VPBank, VIB and ACB are below 30%.
TVN view

Conclusion: positive for heavily indebted companies and real estate, neutral to slightly negative for state-owned banks' margins; hold private banks with cheap deposits, and do not add state-owned banks until Q4 results show margins have held up; this conclusion changes if the State Bank cuts policy rates, in which case the whole sector would be compensated on funding costs.

  • The mechanism here is rate cuts by directive rather than by cheap money: the State Bank is not injecting additional liquidity but is using credit quotas as the enforcement tool, so the gap between the 1.5-2 percentage point lending rate cut and the minimum 0.5 percentage point deposit rate cut must be absorbed by bank margins.
  • An everyday example: a shop is told to cut its selling price by VND 2,000 while its purchase price falls by only VND 500, and the VND 1,500 difference is lost profit.
  • As a result, the banks hit hardest are those with a high share of production and business lending, such as VietinBank at 66% and MB at 57%; those less affected are private banks with heavy retail lending and a high share of demand deposits, such as Techcombank and VPBank, below 30%.
  • What the market has not fully priced in is quota risk: VDSC estimates that SHB, TPBank and Techcombank have exceeded or are about to exceed their 2026 credit quotas when L/Cs are included, so in Q4 these banks may have to slow disbursement, just as the market expects credit to grow quickly.
  • Milestones to watch: data on the average rate for new transactions after the 11-20/10 period and Q3 business results of the state-owned banks; if net interest margins narrow by more than 0.3 percentage points versus Q2, reduce the weighting of this group.

Amended Land Law

removes specific land prices, tightens rules on idle land

TakeawayConclusion: conditionally positive for real estate developers with clean land banks, as land use fees become easier to forecast; hold existing real estate stocks, but do not add yet as the new law is still at the discussion stage; this conclusion changes if the passed bill retains the specific land price mechanism or is postponed to a later session.

Source: Dan tri 22/10, Cong Thuong 22/10, Bao Tin tuc 22/10 (schedule of the 2nd Session of the 16th National Assembly).

  • On 22/10, the 16th National Assembly held group discussions on three bills, the amended Land Law, the amended Housing Law and the amended Real Estate Business Law, following the presentation of the submissions and verification reports; they are expected to be passed at the 2nd Session, with no effective date announced yet.
  • Under the draft amended Land Law, the State will remove the provisions on specific land prices and land valuation consultancy organisations, keeping the land price table and adjustment coefficients as valuation tools; the Economic and Financial Committee, headed by Chairman Phan Văn Mãi, proposed that the land price table be allowed to both rise and fall.
  • For project land that is unused or used behind schedule, the draft imposes a charge that rises progressively with the duration of the violation and provides that if an investor fails to put land into use for more than 60 months, the State will reclaim it; people whose residential land is reclaimed will be compensated with land of the same purpose, other land or cash, and resettled on site.
  • The verifying body required that land reclamation in the national and public interest be genuinely necessary and proposed a review of cases of land reclamation for business purposes.
TVN view

Conclusion: conditionally positive for real estate developers with clean land banks, as land use fees become easier to forecast; hold existing real estate stocks, but do not add yet as the new law is still at the discussion stage; this conclusion changes if the passed bill retains the specific land price mechanism or is postponed to a later session.

  • Specific land pricing is a separate valuation for each project, with an appraisal each time, and is therefore the bottleneck that has delayed land use fee calculations for hundreds of projects; replacing it with the land price table plus coefficients is like moving from haggling over each item to selling at a posted price list.
  • The clearest second-round effect is on timelines: companies with projects awaiting land use fee calculations will see waiting times shortened, but companies holding land with slow development will face progressive charges and the risk of reclamation after 60 months.
  • What the market has not yet noticed is the proposal that the land price table can rise or fall: if this is kept in the law, land use fees will not rise across the board as in the worst-case expectation, easing cost pressure on commercial housing.
  • The 22/10 session showed the market is not yet convinced: 66 real estate stocks fell an average of 0.10% even though VIC and VHM rose sharply.
  • On the advisory side, listed real estate companies should review their project portfolios by the length of land use delays so they can disclose progressive charge risks in advance and avoid surprising shareholders.

TVN view

Conclusion: the channel carrying most of today's impact is portfolio capital flows, and it is working in Vietnam's favour, but only in a handful of large caps; maintain allocation, favour stocks that foreign investors are buying with real money, and avoid technology stocks being sold in line with Asia; the conclusion changes if the Nasdaq falls by more than another 2% on the night of 22/10 and foreign investors return to net selling of more than VND 500 bn.

  • Where the Vietnamese market is mispricing is in treating the rally in VIC and VHM as a market-wide signal: in reality, foreign investors are reallocating from Asian technology into a few large caps with little exposure to chips, while most stocks are not benefiting.
  • What has not yet been priced in is the interest rate differential: the VND overnight rate below the USD rate, oil at USD 111 and a roughly 90% probability of a Fed hike in December all indicate that the domestic rate cuts are unlikely to extend into an easing cycle.
